AI for Maths

News on artificial intelligence and mathematics: what is happening, in brief, and where to read more. Collected by WINDSMATH.

Report · Oct 2026

Postgraduates and supervisors weigh in on AI

The Martingale Foundation surveyed masters and PhD students, and academics and supervisors, in October 2026, collecting more than 260 responses. Its briefing note looks at how rapid developments in AI are changing mathematical research, weighs the threats against the opportunities, and asks what support would help postgraduates most.

Summit · Sep 2026

Mathematicians rethink the PhD

At a two-day summit, a group of mathematicians set out recommendations for PhD programmes: new training in collaboration, communication, AI tools and formalisation; assessment based on in-person written or oral exams; access to AI tools for every graduate student, but never mandatory; and three ethical rules: take responsibility for the mathematics under your name, always disclose AI use, and get agreement before entering anyone else’s material into an AI system.

Essay · Aug 2026

Terence Tao asks what mathematics is for

In an essay adapted from his public lecture at the 2026 International Congress of Mathematicians, Terence Tao sets aside whether AI tools will become capable of research-level problems. He asks instead how the community should prepare, and what mathematical research is fundamentally for, using problem-solving as a case study.

Feature · Jun 2026

Tool or collaborator? Mathematicians on their future

As AI produces proofs and research results, mathematicians are reconsidering the purpose and future of their discipline. IEEE Spectrum gathers views ranging from AI as a simple tool to AI as a collaborator, alongside concerns about unequal access, weakened incentives and students developing fewer skills of their own.

Feature · Apr 2026

From olympiad problems to new research

After strong results at the 2025 International Mathematical Olympiad, AI models began helping mathematicians produce new research results, from the proof of a decades-old optimisation conjecture to newly discovered structures in permutation groups. Quanta also reports the worries: low-quality AI-generated submissions reaching journals, and students relying on AI instead of learning to solve problems.

Essay · Mar 2026

Keeping pace with machines that prove theorems

AI systems can now prove advanced theorems, both in formal proof systems and in ordinary informal reasoning. Jeremy Avigad urges mathematicians to keep pace with these tools, anticipate how they will reshape mathematical work, and adapt to the challenges and opportunities that follow.

Paper · Aug 2025

AI as a copilot, not a replacement

Surveying publicly available language models, Jonas Henkel finds the best of them strong at solving problems and evaluating proofs, but prone to skipping checks on their own reasoning and to reaching correct answers without fully valid proofs. He proposes a human-led workflow, five principles across seven stages of research, in which AI augments the mathematician.
